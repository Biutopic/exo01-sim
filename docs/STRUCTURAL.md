# EXO-01 coupling — note de calcul : descente de charges et liaisons

**Phase 1 of 2. This is a load note, not a structural verification.** It resolves every coupling connection into
a design force with a direction, sizes the connection hardware, and specifies what each attachment must be able
to carry. It does **not** verify a single member, because the project holds no frame sections, no material
specification and no laminate schedule — `config missing_inputs` already records that gap. Phase 2 (member and
laminate verification) needs the inputs listed in §7 and cannot be done from what exists today.

Generated by `scripts/build_structural_note.py` from `results/coupling/connection_loads.json` (2026-11-08). Every figure is computed; none is typed in.

## 1. Scope, and one defect that limits it

The loads come from the coupled two-body static solver (nine thrust-derived load cases) and the linear
frequency-domain response in the three Erdre reference waves. Two limits matter more than anything else here:

1. **The time-domain snatch load is not bounded by the model.** §14 showed the tension-only peaks wander between
   11,0 and 15,7 kN across time steps, with no element damping anywhere in the model. So the dynamic allowance
   below (×2.5) is **engineering judgement from mooring practice, not a computed number.** Measuring a real
   snatch load is the single change that would most reduce the uncertainty in this note.
2. **A known equilibrium defect quarantines the two differential-thrust cases.** A fender with a declared contact
   axis applies a spurious couple of cross(a−b, f) — measured at 178 N·m from one bumper at 2 652 N. The
   surge-only cases are unaffected (force residual 0,02 N, moment residual 0,0–1,8 N·m). The yaw cases
   `diff_one` and `diff_opp` carry a 0,5 % error in this arrangement and up to 7,6 % in the brief's own
   configuration. The couple is **restoring**, so modelled yaw is too small and any preload set by a yaw case is
   **underestimated** — non-conservative. See COUPLING.md §16. Loads below that are governed by a yaw case are
   marked; treat them as lower bounds.

## 5. Connections — how to make each one

The absolute loads are small: nothing exceeds about 800 N characteristic. **Strength is not the design driver —
fatigue, chafe, pull-out and stiffness are.** Everything below follows from that.

### 5.1 Boat side — the critical unknown

Every boat-side load lands on an existing cleat, and **no cleat on Hydronaute has a known rating.** A design load
of 2 006 N is trivial for a through-bolted cleat on a backing
plate and entirely capable of tearing out a cleat that is screwed into a thin deck laminate. This is the one
item that must be resolved before any trial.

- **Verify or refit every cleat used**: through-bolted, stainless fasteners, with a backing plate or load-
  spreading pad under the deck, not screws into the laminate. Pull-out, not shear, is the failure mode.
- **Orient for the actual direction.** The midship spring pulls at roughly `[0.87, 0.49, 0.08]` — nearly
  horizontal and about 30° off fore-and-aft. A cleat handles that; a padeye must be set in that plane or it
  bends.
- **Soft shackles or spliced eyes, not S-hooks**, on anything that cycles. An open hook on the element whose
  failure loses reverse restraint is the wrong terminal.
- **Chafe protection where the crossed ropes cross the deck edge.** They lie across the aft deck by geometry
  (§15.1), which is ordinary practice but needs a rubbing strip or a lead.

### 5.2 EXO side

- **Rear bumper mounts** at `[2.10, ±0.90, 0.25]`: pure fore-and-aft compression, direction `[-1, 0, 0]`. Load
  the crossmember in line; avoid a cantilevered pad bracket, which turns an axial load into a bending moment at
  the weld.
- **Forward posts** at `[4.95, ±2.25, 0.30]` and **mid posts** at `[2.10, ±2.25, 0.30]`: these take rope loads
  at an angle, so the lug must be a padeye in the load plane with a gusset into the post, not a tab welded to a
  tube wall. A tube loaded by a surface-welded tab fails by wall tearing well below the tab's own rating.
- **Dockwheel brackets**: carry **0 N in every static case** in this arrangement. Size them as a physical stop
  and for handling. §15.4 also found the 30 mm gap is not a dimension needing precision.

### 5.3 Hardware that matches these loads

Against the largest textile design load of 1 901 N:

- **12 mm polyester double braid** (MBL typically 25–30 kN) — comfortably inside the 5:1 requirement, and §14
  already chose it over the 4,8 mm UHMWPE on stiffness grounds. Stay with it.
- **A 2" (50 mm) retractable ratchet strap** (MBL 13,5 kN, WLL 4,5 kN) also satisfies 5:1 for the springs and
  the crossed ropes, which makes it a legitimate choice for those two pairs — with an inline dynamometer, because
  §5–§7 showed rope tension is what sets the rear compression exactly, and a ratchet handle reads nothing. Treat
  the webbing as an annually replaced consumable: its rating is a land-cargo rating, not a marine cyclic one.
- **Shackles**: any 8–10 mm stainless bow shackle clears the 4:1 metallic requirement at these loads. Size by
  the pin and eye geometry, not by rating.

## 7. What phase 2 needs, and what would change these numbers

**Required before any member or laminate check:**

1. EXO frame drawings: section sizes, wall thicknesses, material and temper, and the joint details at every
   attachment point above.
2. Hydronaute's deck laminate schedule and the construction of each cleat mounting, or a pull test on one.
3. The float-to-frame connection — the model assumes the floats and frame move as one rigid body and
   `missing_inputs` flags the stiffness as unknown.

**Would materially change the loads in this note:**

4. **The pods' bollard thrust.** §15.5 found `diff_opp` binds the preload of thirteen of seventeen arrangements,
   and it rests on a 1 500 N order-of-magnitude guess. Measure it and every preload in this note tightens.
5. **A measured snatch load**, which would replace the ×2,5 judgement with a number.
6. **The three contact stiffnesses** (bumper, wheel, rope), still unmeasured, which spread the quasi-static
   answer by a factor of 3 (§7) even though rope tension pins it exactly.
7. **The fender-couple defect** (§1.2), which must be fixed before the yaw-governed loads can be trusted.
